1. What SCUDEM actually is

An international, team-based mathematical modeling competition. You get a real-world scenario, build a differential equation (or difference equation) model of it, and present your solution in a 10-minute video. There's no exam and no single right answer.

« Organizer: COMAP (a nonprofit), with support from MathWorks (the MATLAB company). Past problems and winning videos live on the SIMIODE YouTube channel. « Name: Gemini said "SIMIODE Challenge Using Differential Equations Modeling." The official FAQ spells it "Student Challenge Using Differential Equations Modeling." Both names are in use, which is a historical quirk, so don't worry about it. + Who can enter: undergraduate or high school students. Teammates can be from different schools.

1. The rule that matters most

After the challenge begins, student team members may not receive help from living beings, including the coach.

Before Oct 16, your coach can help you prepare: past problems, modeling advice, anything. After the problems drop, nobody outside your team can help you. That includes friends,

seniors, professors and online forums where a human answers you. Tools (software, Al, textbooks, papers) are fine.

What a strong video probably contains

Roughly following the rubric: the problem in one sentence, your assumptions (and why), the model, how you solved it, results with clear plots and units, a sanity check, limitations and reflection, and a conclusion that answers the actual question. Cut anything that doesn't serve that story, because the rubric explicitly penalizes superfluous material.

1. The pitch for your teammate

SCUDEM is a modeling sprint, not a speed-math exam. On Oct 16 we get three real-world problems. We pick one, build a differential-equations model of it, and test it in code. Then we record a 10-minute video explaining what we did and why. Nobody outside our team can help us once it starts, but we can use whatever software we want. Judges give feedback and everyone gets a certificate tier. It's good applied-math practice, and a strong video looks good on a CV. Registration closes this Friday, it's $100 per team, and we need a coach.
